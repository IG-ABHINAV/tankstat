# User guide

For people who use Tankstat: what is on the screen and how the features behave. Operators find the settings in [Configuration](configuration.md).

## First start (setup)

A new server opens on its setup page instead of the app, until it has its first administrator (or, with sign-in off, until someone confirms that everyone may see and change everything). The page asks for the **setup code** the server wrote to its log as it started; whoever runs the server finds it there (`docker logs` with Docker), and every start writes a new one. With the code, a wizard takes you through the server's settings, a step per group of the *Server settings* page (choose how people sign in first; every later step can be skipped), and ends by creating the administrator with an e-mail address and a password, by making you the administrator when you signed in through an identity provider or a proxy (the notice at the top of the app links to the page then), or, with sign-in off, with the confirmation; the server then restarts once when a setting needs it. To bring a server back instead, choose *Restore from a backup*: the page then lists the backups in the server's backups folder (a backup file from your device, one of the old server, say, can be uploaded there first), and restoring one brings back its users, data and server settings. An administrator made or chosen there then sees what is worth setting up next: encrypted backups (the key set is made on the spot), the backup schedule, the defaults for new vehicles and the other users. The setup itself never shows again, unless a server with local accounts has no administrator left (it opens again, to make one). Servers whose administrators the environment names, or whose setup it turns off, skip it. Details for operators: [Authentication](authentication.md#the-first-start-setup).

## Home

The app is mobile-first and responsive. The start page (*Home*) shows a card per vehicle you own or that is shared with you (a search box finds vehicles by name or license plate, the server does the searching, and more cards load as you scroll to the end of the list; a *Show more* button does the same without scrolling), and is where you add vehicles. *Arrange* (shown from two vehicles on; it lists up to 200 vehicles) puts the cards in your own order, saved with your account: the vehicles you arranged come first, the rest follow by name. Each card shows: its picture as the background (a gradient without one), plate and fuel type, a badge when the vehicle is shared with you, the latest odometer, average consumption, last fill-up, this month's spending (in every currency it was spent in, e.g. "52,000 Ft · €73.90": amounts are never converted) and a small six-month trend in the main currency, and what is overdue or due soon among the vehicle's recurring expenses. If you may add logs to the vehicle, the card also has quick actions: **Refuel** and **Expense** open the same dialogs as the vehicle page, and the tick next to a schedule that needs attention opens **Mark as done**, all without leaving *Home*; afterwards only that card is refreshed, in place. People who may only view the vehicle's logs see no buttons.

## Navigation

A navigation menu opened by the hamburger button in the top bar gives access to:

- Everyone sees and manages their own vehicles on *Home* (editing and deleting appear for vehicles you may edit).
- **Vehicle page** (tabs; a banner with the vehicle's picture on top): **Dashboard** (key figures, ready-made charts and your own charts), **Refuelings** (the logs: add, edit, move to trash; sortable, paged, selectable columns; in the add and edit dialogs any two of volume, price per unit and total give the third (total = volume × price per unit − discount; litres worked out from the amount paid and the pump's price are off by a discount you did not type); once all three are filled, a change moves the amount the app worked out, otherwise the price per unit, otherwise the total (when it is the price per unit you changed, unless you typed the total or a photo read it: then the discount is worked out from the three), while litres you typed, saved or got from a photo never change by themselves: type the litres, the amount you paid and the pump's price, and the discount appears (a difference under a tenth of a per cent is the receipt's rounding, not a discount); or the litres, the pump's price and the discount the receipt shows, and the total becomes what you paid; the price per unit is only a help for typing and is not stored, the discount is, and two optional columns show it and the price per unit before it), **Expenses** (service, insurance, parking, ...: title, free-text category, a cost with its own currency and an optional discount (the amount is what was paid; an optional column shows the discount), optional odometer; same access rules as the logs), **Recurring** (schedules that come back, see [Recurring expenses](#recurring-expenses)), **Details** (owner, fuel, units, the vehicle's picture) and **Sharing** (give people access to this vehicle's logs; only for those who can edit the vehicle).
- **Import**: bring fuel logs, other costs and recurring expenses from another app (Fuelio CSV today), see [Importing](importing.md).
- **Notifications** (also the bell in the top bar, which shows how many are unread and the latest ones): what you were told, newest first; mark one or all read. See [Notifications](#notifications).
- **Trash**: deleting moves a vehicle, a refuelling or an expense to the trash. Tabs *Vehicles*, *Refuelings* and *Expenses*: **restore**, or **empty the trash**, which permanently deletes only what you have Delete access to (the rest stays and the dialog says so).
- **Account** (profile picture; change password in Standalone mode; **Signed-in devices** in Standalone and OIDC mode: every browser or installed app you are signed in on, with when it signed in and was last used, *This device* marked; **Sign out** one of them, or **Sign out everywhere else** after a confirmation; **Offline data**: what your devices download for use without the server, see [Install as an app](install-as-an-app.md#when-the-server-is-out-of-reach)), **Install app** (when your browser can install the web app, see [Install as an app](install-as-an-app.md)), plus **Sign out**.

Every **password field** (the sign-in, a password change, the server's secrets, a backup's passphrase) has a **Show** button of its own. What you typed shows until you click it again, leave the field, send the form or stop typing for 30 seconds; it is hidden again whenever the page or dialog opens. The field also says when Caps Lock is on. A **new password** is typed twice: when the two differ, the form says so at the second one and sends nothing. Under it the field says how long a password must be (an administrator can raise the minimum, *Minimum password length* in the sign-in settings), and a shorter one is stopped there. Once you type, it also says how strong the password is, from very weak to strong, and what makes it weak (a common password, your own name or address, repeated characters, runs such as abc or 123, a keyboard row, one kind of character only), with a bar beside the words. That is advice only: a weak password that is long enough is still saved. A few unrelated words make a strong password that is easy to remember. If the minimum was raised after you chose your password, you still sign in with it, but the app then shows only **Choose a new password** (and Sign out) until you have changed it; nothing else of yours changes.

Administrators also get an **Administration** group at the end of the menu (never with `Auth:Mode=None`, where there is no administrator):

- **Overview** (`/admin`): what needs your attention on the server, such as saved settings waiting for a restart or a settings file the last start left out, each with a link to where you deal with it, a few facts (version, database, how many users and vehicles), and the server's **folders** (the pictures, the photos of logs, the backups, the sign-in keys and the settings file) in full, each with the environment variable that sets it: the settings page leaves folders out, since they are where the environment mounts its disks. When nothing needs you it says so in one line.
- **People** (`/admin/people`): the users (add, edit, reset links, administrator rights, disabling, deleting) and the access to other people's data, in two tabs.
- **Vehicles** (`/admin/vehicles`): the grid of all vehicles of everyone, sorted and paged by the server, with edit and delete.
- **Server settings** (`/admin/settings`, see below).
- **Backups** (`/admin/backups`): back up now, upload, download and restore backups, see [Backup and restore](backup-and-restore.md#on-the-administration-page).

The older addresses still work: `/vehicles` leads to the vehicle grid, and `/admin?tab=access` to the access tab.

On a desktop the menu is a docked sidebar, open by default; the hamburger button hides and shows it, and the choice is remembered for you (see [Settings follow you](#settings-follow-you)). On a phone it is an overlay drawer, closed until the button is pressed, and closes after choosing a page; it is never remembered.

## Recurring expenses

The *Recurring* tab of a vehicle holds schedules for things that come back: insurance every 12 months, an oil change every 15,000 km or every 12 months, whichever comes first. A schedule repeats by **time**, by **distance (odometer)** or **combined** (whichever is reached first), counts from the day (and odometer) it was last done, and shows when it is next due and whether it is *Upcoming*, *Due soon* (within the warning time and distance of the item; a new schedule starts from `Defaults:RecurringWarnDays` / `Defaults:RecurringWarnDistance`, 30 days and 500 distance units unless configured) or *Overdue*. The current odometer is the vehicle's latest reading from any log; without one a distance-based schedule cannot be judged yet. **Mark as done** covers one service visit: it lists the vehicle's schedules, the one you started from and the others of its category that are due soon or overdue already ticked (an oil change with its oil and air filters, while the fuel filter keeps its own interval and the vignette in *Fees* is not part of the service; categories match whatever their case, but accents count: *Szerviz* is not *Szervíz*). A schedule without a category starts alone. Anything else can be ticked by hand. Saving starts the next interval of each ticked one from the day and odometer you enter. The amount is optional and covers all of them together, never split: with an amount (what was paid, and an optional discount the workshop took off, kept with it) one normal expense is logged for the visit (so every expense and odometer rule applies), its title and category prefilled from the schedules and editable, and it remembers which schedules it covered (the Expenses grid's *Recurring* column, hidden until you show it); without an amount only the schedules move on. It is all or nothing: if one ticked schedule cannot be done on that day or at that odometer, the message names it and nothing is saved. Photos taken there (the dashboard, the invoice) become the expense's photos; they are kept only when an expense is logged, that is with an amount, or while a photo is still being read and may give the amount. The *Recurring* tab can also tick several schedules and mark them done together (*Mark selected as done*). Cost reports count such an expense once, under its category. Schedules follow the access rules of the vehicle's logs (View sees them, Edit changes them) and are deleted for good, not trashed; deleting a user hands them on or removes them like the vehicle's other data. What is overdue or due soon is listed on the vehicle's card on *Home*.

## Notifications

You are notified when someone gives you access to a vehicle's logs, changes or takes it away; when someone other than you shares *your* vehicle; when an administrator gives you (or someone, on your data) access to everything a user owns; when an administrator changes the default access for everyone; and when a recurring expense of a vehicle whose logs you may see becomes due soon or overdue (an administrator only for vehicles that concern them personally, not for every vehicle on the instance). Administrators are also told when a backup the app was to write failed (once a day at most; see [Backup and restore](backup-and-restore.md#schedule-and-retention)). Recurring reminders are worked out when you look (the bell asks every minute while the page is open), once per cycle of the schedule: one that turns from due soon to overdue is the same notification again, unread, and marking it done starts a new cycle. To avoid floods, changes to the same thing are folded into the unread notification about it ("3 changes"), a change undone before you read it disappears, and past `Notifications:MaxPerHour` notifications in an hour further ones are only counted in one "more changes" notification. You cannot delete notifications: you mark them read (when is kept), and the system removes them `Notifications:ReadRetentionDays` after that (checked when you look at them; a reminder of a schedule that is still due stays). Nobody else, administrators included, can see yours. With `Auth:Mode=None` they work for the single anonymous user.

## Appearance: colour mode and surfaces

The sun/moon button in the top bar, *Appearance* (next to the language menu; on a phone both are at the bottom of the navigation menu, where there is room), holds two choices:

- **Colour mode:** *Light*, *Dark* (the default) or *System* (follows your device). Vehicle cards and banners stay dark in either mode, since their white text sits on a dark scrim.
- **Surfaces:** how the floating panels are drawn (the top bar, the navigation, dialogs, menus, cards, tables, the short messages at the bottom of the screen, and the figures over a vehicle card's picture):
  - *Glossy* (the default): see-through and blurred.
  - *Transparent*: see-through, not blurred.
  - *Opaque*: solid, so nothing behind a panel shows through.

Both are available before sign-in and saved with your account once you are signed in. The browser's own copy is applied before the first paint, so there is no flash; on a device that has never seen your choice they switch as soon as the account's setting arrives.

## Saved, undone, added from anywhere

Saving a log, vehicle, schedule or chart ends with a short "Saved." message. Moving a vehicle, refuelling or expense to the trash (after the usual confirmation) offers *Undo* for ten seconds. On a phone, a floating add button on the vehicle page logs a refuelling or an expense from any tab. Dates are picked with a date picker (typing works too; a date in the future is refused), the currency and the expense category suggest values as you type, and the Recurring tab shows a small dial per limit of each schedule (a combined schedule has two; the one that decides is the stronger).

## Settings follow you

What the UI remembers is stored with your account, so another device shows the same once you have signed in there: whether the sidebar is open, each grid's columns, their order, the page size and the sort, the language, the time and date formats, the colour mode, the surfaces and the order of your vehicles on *Home*. The browser keeps a copy of every setting (`localStorage`: `tankstat.nav.open`, `tankstat.grid.<grid>`, `tankstat.language`, `tankstat.colorMode`, `tankstat.surface`, `tankstat.formats`), so the page paints at once; the server is asked once per session and its answer wins, except for a setting you changed meanwhile, and a setting the server does not know keeps the browser's copy; the time and date formats follow the account exactly, *Default* included, so a format chosen before signing in (or while the server could not be reached) gives way to the account's at the next session. A save that fails (the server is unreachable) only shows in the browser console; the setting still applies in that browser. With `Auth:Mode=None` every visitor shares the anonymous user's settings and arrangement. Deleting a user removes their settings; they are never handed to someone else. The tables are `UiSettings`, `GridSettings` and `VehicleOrders`; the GraphQL fields are `uiSettings`, `updateUiSettings`, `saveGridSettings`, `resetGridSettings` and `setVehicleOrder`. Two devices saving the same setting at the same moment: the later save wins as a whole (the sidebar, the language, the formats, the colour mode and the surfaces are one record, each grid another).

## Server settings (administrators)

**Server settings** in the Administration group shows what the server runs with, a tab per group: sign-in, e-mail, defaults (what new vehicles and schedules start with, and the time and date formats of everyone who chose none), notifications, offline sync, photo reading, backups, logging and telemetry (the address remembers the tab, `?tab=smtp`, and a tab with changes not saved yet says so). A group with several kinds of settings is folded into sections (sign-in: sessions, Standalone, OIDC, reverse proxy, administrators from the provider); what decides the rest, the sign-in mode or the photo reading provider, stays above them. Settings that are not used with the current choices, such as the OIDC settings while sign-in is off or the mail server's port while there is no mail server, are still shown with their values but cannot be changed, and their section folds and says why; the choice counts as soon as it is made, before saving. Each setting shows beside its label where its value comes from (the environment, the settings file, `appsettings.json` or the built-in default), and a setting nothing sets shows the value it runs with. The question mark next to it says what it does and which environment variable sets it. One an environment variable sets is read-only: change it there. The others are saved into the server's own settings file. Each tab says when its changes take effect: the defaults, notifications, offline sync, backups and logging as soon as they are saved; sign-in, e-mail and photo reading after a restart, which the page offers (*Restart server*: the server is back within seconds and the page reloads), except *Minimum password length* on the sign-in tab, which counts for the next new password as soon as it is saved and is marked *Applies at once*. Only a setting that really waits for a restart is marked *Restart needed*, and the message after a save says which it was for what that save changed. A secret (a password, a client secret, an API key) is never shown back, only whether it is set; leave it blank to keep it, tick *Remove the stored value* to drop it. A change of the sign-in mode asks once more: at that restart everybody is signed out, you included, and if you cannot sign in afterwards, the environment variable `Auth__Mode` wins over the file. What the server could not take is listed under the form, and nothing of that save is kept. The page needs a connection to the server; the file and the rules are in [Configuration](configuration.md#the-apps-own-settings-file).

## Dashboard and charts

The first tab of a vehicle shows key figures (spent this month and last month, in every currency used, never converted; average consumption of the last ten full fill-ups, odometer, last fill-up), five ready-made charts (monthly costs split into fuel and expenses, consumption, fuel price, expenses by category, distance per month) and the charts people composed, as the dashboard is arranged (see [Customizing the dashboard](#customizing-the-dashboard)); until someone arranges it, the **Standard** preset shows exactly these, the composed charts after the ready-made ones. *Add widget* while customizing the dashboard (below) opens, under *Blank chart*, a builder for a chart of your own: what to measure (total spend, fuel cost, expense cost, fuel volume, distance, average consumption, average fuel price paid, average fuel price before discounts, number of fill-ups, and the cost per distance: what was spent per 100 km or 100 miles driven, fuel and expenses together or fuel alone, per currency), how to group it (month, quarter, year, or expense category for expense costs), the chart type (bars, line, area, donut) and the period: 1 month, 3 months, half a year, one year, this year, last year, all time, or a **custom from-to range**. The measures are lists with an icon per entry; the rest are rows of icon buttons (a tooltip names each, and the chosen one is written beside the row's name). What a choice rules out stays in place, dimmed, its reason in the tooltip, and a live preview shows your own data. A chart is private to its creator unless someone with edit access to the vehicle's logs shares it with everyone who sees the vehicle; only the creator changes it (anyone with delete access may remove a shared one). The numbers are calculated by the server from the stored logs (costs per currency, never mixed), charts are drawn with MUI X Charts, and every chart's numbers open as a table in a dialog from the button in its card's header (which is what screen readers read). A chart of costs in **several currencies draws each currency on a scale of its own**: the highest month of each reaches the same height, whatever the size of its numbers, and every scale starts at zero (a price that barely moves stays a flat line), so there is no value axis and a line under the chart says so; the amounts are in the tooltip and the table, where a month without spending in a currency shows a dash. A month's bars are only those of the currencies paid in it, side by side in the middle of the month. With one currency the axis shows its amounts in that currency. The months along the bottom are short, and as many are named as fit (on a phone every second or third); the year is under the first one named and under the first one named in each new year, and the tooltip names every month in full.

A chart over time (by month, quarter or year; not a donut or a chart by category) can also show where its values are heading, all worked out by the server and listed in the chart's table: a **trend line**, either the *moving average* of the last three periods or a *linear trend* (a straight line fitted to the values; the chart says in words where it goes, from its first period to its last); a **running total**, each period showing the sum so far (only for what adds up: costs, volume, distance, fill-ups; it has no trend line); and **a year earlier**, the same periods one year before as a line of their own (the same days: a chart of this year so far is compared with last year up to the same day). These are always lines over the chart, in the colour of the series they belong to and on its scale (each currency on its own, so a year earlier is read against this year): a trend dashed, a year earlier dotted and fainter. For what adds up, a trend leaves out a period that is not whole: the one still running, and one the period cuts at its edge (a custom range's first and last month, the first period of all time), since half a month's costs would pull it down; averages such as consumption and price keep them. A fitted line never goes below zero. With fewer than three periods to go by there is no trend yet, and a year with nothing logged has no line; the chart says so. A chart of costs per distance has no value where nothing was driven in a period, and distance counts only the part of a period inside the chart's range, as costs do. A chart over time can also have a **second measure**: another metric in the same chart, as bars, a line or an area, on a scale of its own (distance as bars with consumption as a line, say). The first measure's scale is on the left and the second's on the right, each shown when its measure has one scale; when both are in the same unit and currency they share one. The trend line, the running total and the year earlier are the first measure's; the first measure's split into fuel and expenses never takes the second measure's bars into its stack. In the table the second measure's column is named after its metric, each written in its own unit.

### Customizing the dashboard

The dashboard is a grid of tiles: the key figures and the charts, four columns wide on a desktop (two on a tablet, one on a phone, in the same reading order). *Customize* arranges it in place: drag a tile by the strip across its top, resize it by its bottom-right corner, or open the strip's menu (the ⋮ button, which also works with a keyboard and a screen reader): *Move earlier* and *Move later* in the reading order, *Wider*, *Narrower*, *Taller*, *Shorter*, *Pin* (a pinned tile is never pushed aside by dragging the others) and *Remove*; on a ready-made chart it starts with *Customize as my chart…*, which opens the builder with the chart's recipe and title: once anything differs (*Save as my chart* stays off until then), it is saved as a chart of your own that takes the ready-made tile's place, size and pin, and the ready-made one is offered in *Add widget* again. *Add widget* opens a dialog with everything that can go on it: the key figures not on it, every ready-made chart (one already on the dashboard marked *On the dashboard*: that one can only be customized there, and the chart of your own it becomes goes at the bottom, beside it), your charts taken off it, and *Blank chart*; the one you pick shows with its preview on your data and *Add to the dashboard* puts it at the bottom. A trend figure's preview has its period under it (the same controls as its chip, below), so it goes on at the period you choose. A ready-made chart goes on as it is, or *Customize this chart* unfolds the builder with its recipe: once anything differs, *Save and add* saves it as a chart of your own and adds that, the ready-made one still offered. On everyone's dashboard a chart made there is shared, since a private one would be dropped from it. Editing or deleting one of your own charts is done while customizing too, from the pencil and the bin on its tile; outside *Customize* a tile shows only its content. *Start from a preset* replaces the tiles with a preset's. Nothing is kept until *Save dashboard*; *Cancel* leaves it as it was (a chart you saved meanwhile stays saved and shows at the first free spot). A tile can be as narrow as one column (a chart at least five rows tall), as wide as the grid, and up to 24 rows tall. A key figure shows what its rows hold: two rows its label and value, three its comparison or note as well, four its little chart too; every figure can be made as low as two rows. Nothing is cut: what has no room in a narrow tile is left out from the bottom (the little chart, then the note), and a label that still does not fit is shortened with "…" (its whole text shows on hover, on focus or on a tap). A trend figure's comparison that has no room (always at two rows) is its arrow alone before the value, still pointing up or down in its colour; hovering, focusing or tapping it shows the sentence, and outside *Customize* the period under it. A value too wide for its line gets smaller step by step, down to the size of body text, so it fits; *Spent this month* writes its amounts a little smaller when they take more than a line, and when even the smallest size does not fit them on one, it shows the main currency and "+1" (or more), whose tooltip lists the others. The cost per distance and the consumption write their unit smaller beside the number. A trend figure's tile also keeps its period. Arranging on a phone or a tablet keeps the tiles' sizes and saves the new order.

- **Everyone who sees this vehicle**: the vehicle's dashboard, which everyone who can see its logs gets. Changing it needs edit access to the logs. Only shared charts can be on it (a chart only you can see is not; share it to add it there). *Back to Standard* forgets it.
- **Just me**: your own arrangement of the vehicle's dashboard, which wins over it for you alone; anyone who sees the vehicle may make one. It starts from what you see, can hold your private charts, and the dashboard says when you are looking at it: *Use the vehicle's dashboard* goes back. With sign-in off everyone is the same visitor, so only the vehicle's dashboard is offered.

Three presets: **Standard** is the dashboard as it always was (the four key figures in a row, then the five ready-made charts two by two); **Spending** and **Usage** put the trend figures and the charts that go with them on it. The **trend figures** say where things are heading, each with an arrow and words, never by colour alone (the arrow is coloured by what the change means: spending or fuel going up is red, going down green; distance is neutral either way): *Spending* (in the main currency, other currencies named under it), *Cost per distance change* (money per 100 km or 100 miles driven; it says so when nothing was driven in the period), *Consumption change* (the mean of the stored consumptions in the period, as soon as there is one, with how many fill-ups each side holds: a comparison needs 3 earlier fill-ups; worded as uses more or less fuel, so it reads right in miles per gallon too) and *Distance driven*.

Each trend figure looks at a **period of its own**. While customizing, a chip on the tile's strip names it (outlined, so it is seen and pressed); outside *Customize* the card shows no chip, and its arrow, when it stands alone, says the period in its tooltip. By default it is what the figure always showed: the last 12 full months against the 12 before for *Spending* and *Cost per distance change* (this month is left out, since it is still running, which is why they say *full months*: the *Last 12 months* chart range includes it), the last 10 fill-ups against the 10 before for *Consumption change*, and this year so far against last year up to the same day for *Distance driven*. While customizing, the chip opens the period:

- **Quick**: the figure's usual picks (3, 6 or 12 full months; 5, 10 or 20 fill-ups; this year or 12 full months).
- **Last**: a number of days, weeks, months, years or fill-ups. The period ends today, unless weeks, months or years are made *whole*: Monday to Sunday, calendar months or calendar years, the running one left out. A number of fill-ups runs from the oldest of them to today.
- **Since**: a first day (from 2000 on); the period runs to today.
- **Compared with**: *the period before* (as long, or as many fill-ups, right before it) or *the same dates a year earlier* (the same days one year back, 29 February becoming 28 February).

A line shows the two periods with their dates, and the figure as the tile will show it follows as you choose; the chip says the comparison too when it is not the figure's usual one (*Last 8 weeks · vs a year earlier*). *Apply* puts the period into the arrangement being edited, and *Save dashboard* keeps it, with the rest. Five more **ready-made charts** go with them: *Spent so far this year* (a running total, with last year beside it), *Cost per distance* (with a moving average), *Consumption trend* (by quarter, all time, with a linear trend), *Distance, this year and last*, and *Distance and consumption* (bars and a line). A ready-made chart becomes a chart of your own from its tile's menu or through *Add widget* (above), the original staying available. A saved chart the arrangement does not mention (one made after it) takes the first free spot, so a new chart is never lost; a chart taken off stays saved and can be added back. Deleting a chart takes it off every arrangement. Arranging needs the server; offline, the dashboard shows as it was last seen.

## Fuel consumption

Each full fill-up shows the consumption since the previous full one: all fuel added since then (partial top-ups in between count, the previous full fill-up does not) divided by the distance driven, per 100 distance units, in the vehicle's own units (for example L/100 km; miles with gallons read as mpg). Partial fill-ups, the first full fill-up and logs without any distance since the last full one show a dash. When you forgot to log a fill-up, switch on *Missed fill-up before this one* on the next log you enter (the grid marks it *Missed one before*): the fuel of the forgotten fill-up is unknown, so that interval shows a dash too and the calculation starts again from there, instead of showing a consumption that is too low. It is stored on the log and refreshed for the whole vehicle whenever one of its logs is added, changed, trashed, restored or imported (once per import), never on read; the first start after the upgrade calculates it for existing logs. The column can be sorted on the server like the others.

## Units and money

Every vehicle has its own distance unit (kilometres or miles) and fuel volume unit (litres, US gallons, imperial gallons); numbers are stored exactly as entered and never converted, so the units are locked once a vehicle has logs. Every cost carries its own currency (a reusable `Cost` value: amount plus currency, and an optional **discount**: what the till took off, so a refuelling can say what was paid, what the pump showed and the price per unit both ways; every total and chart of spending counts what was paid), and a log links to an odometer reading (a reusable `OdometerReading`, without an upper limit; the server checks a new reading against the neighbouring readings of the same vehicle, from any source).

## Pictures

Users can upload a profile picture (Account page; shown wherever users appear) and a picture per vehicle (Details tab). The browser scales the picture down (and crops profile pictures square) and re-encodes it before it is sent; the server still checks the real file type (JPEG, PNG, WebP only; 2 MiB maximum). Pictures are served from `/media/{id}` (immutable, cached; only to signed-in users who may see the owner or vehicle) and uploaded with `PUT /media/me/avatar` and `PUT /media/vehicles/{id}/picture` (removed with `DELETE` on the same paths). These, the photo endpoints below and the import upload (`POST /imports/{format}`) are the only REST endpoints; everything else is GraphQL.

## Photos of refuelings and expenses

Every refueling and expense can have up to 10 photos (receipts, the pump display, ...). In the add and edit dialogs, **Take photo** opens the phone's camera straight away and **Add photos** picks from the library; photos chosen while adding are uploaded straight away as drafts and attached when the entry is saved (so a failed save loses none; a photo that could not be uploaded is marked and can be tried again), and while editing they are uploaded or removed at once. Photos are scaled to 1600 px in the browser (location data in them is dropped), stored in the log's folder below the vehicle's, with the pictures, or in a folder of their own when one is set (see `Storage:PhotosPath` in [Configuration](configuration.md#files-defaults-and-notifications)) and follow the access rules of the vehicle's logs: whoever may see the log may see its photos, whoever may edit it may add and remove them. They are uploaded with `PUT /media/expenses/{id}/photos` or `PUT /media/refuelings/{id}/photos`, removed with `DELETE` on `.../photos/{imageId}` and shown through `/media/{id}`. Drafts are uploaded with `PUT /media/vehicles/{id}/photo-drafts` (needs edit access to the vehicle's logs), removed with `DELETE /media/photo-drafts/{id}`, attached through the `photoIds` of `logRefueling` / `addExpense`, seen only by their uploader, kept in `vehicles/<id>/drafts/` and removed after a day if no entry was saved with them. A photo disappears from view with its log in the trash and is deleted for good when the log or its vehicle is.

## Reading values from photos (optional)

With a [vision model](photo-reading.md) set up, the server reads the photos picked in the add dialogs of refuelings and expenses and in *Mark as done*, and photos added to a saved refueling or expense in its edit dialog (there the saved values stay: a photo only offers what differs, and fills what is empty, so you can clear a value and save while the photo is read): a dashboard gives the odometer, a fuel receipt the date, litres, price per litre, the discount printed for the fuel, total and currency (the total is what was paid for the fuel itself, after the discount, never the receipt's grand total when it also lists a car wash or screen wash; whichever of the amounts it does not show is worked out from the others; a discount comes into a log saved before its photo was read only together with the total it was taken off, never onto an amount you entered), another receipt the date, shop (as the title), amount (after any discount), the discount printed on it and currency. While a photo is being read its thumbnail says *Reading…*; then the values go into the fields you have not changed (the starting values, such as today's date or the last currency, count as unchanged), marked *Read from the photo; check it*. Where you already typed something else, or the app worked the value out from what you typed, the photo's value is only offered (*The photo shows 38.52 · Use it*), never written over yours. Only values the model is sure enough of are filled in (`Recognition:MinConfidence`). The page only talks to Tankstat's own server, which talks to the model server; without one the dialogs work exactly as before.

## Accessibility

The UI is built to be keyboard- and screen-reader friendly: landmarks and a skip link, a labelled navigation, labelled form controls with linked hints and errors, announced upload/loading status, table semantics with `aria-sort`, 44 px touch targets, dialogs with focus management, and reduced-motion support. Automated axe checks run in the frontend integration tests; colour contrast should still be reviewed by eye. The look is dim and layered: translucent blurred panels (top bar, sidebar) with soft shadows. Changes of state move briefly so you can follow them (a message or a value read from a photo comes in, a row leaves a list, rearranged cards slide to their places, waits show a spinner); with reduced motion only fades remain.

## Grids

Sorting (click a column header), paging and column selection are done by the **server**: the GraphQL query gets `orderBy`, `direction`, `skip`, `take`, and one Boolean variable per optional column that drives `@include`, so hidden columns are not even fetched. The toolbar has a refresh button and a column picker (show/hide and move up/down, which works with touch). Column choices, order, page size and sorting are remembered per grid, for you (a copy in the browser and the server's with your account, see [Settings follow you](#settings-follow-you)). The grid is MUI X's Data Grid in server mode: the data itself arrives sorted and paged from the server, a page holds at most 100 rows, and a row you move to the trash fades out while the server removes it. Data is re-fetched whenever a page is opened. The layout is mobile-first: on a phone only the essential columns start visible, and the table scrolls horizontally.

## Languages

English and Hungarian; the language menu in the top bar (on a phone, at the bottom of the navigation menu once signed in) is available before sign-in, defaults to the browser language and is remembered in the browser and, once signed in, with your account (so your other devices switch too; a code the app does not know is ignored). Dates and numbers follow the selected language. The same menu, *Language and formats*, also chooses how **times** (24-hour or 12-hour) and **dates** (2026-10-13, 13.10.2026, 13/10/2026, 10/13/2026 or 2026. 10. 13.) are written, each offered with an example; *Default* writes them as the server's administrator set for everyone (*Server settings*, *Defaults*), else as your language does. The choice applies to the date fields too, and is remembered like the language, except that the account's choice wins even when it is *Default* ([Settings follow you](#settings-follow-you)). Adding a language is described in [Development](development.md#languages).
